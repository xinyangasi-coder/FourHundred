Foundation

A 7-day cash map

Last reviewed September 29, 2026 · 8 minutes

Who this is for. Anyone in the U.S. whose month looks possible on paper and still breaks in a particular week.
Who should skip this. Readers who already cannot cover this month’s essentials, or who only need the $400 definition.

In one sentence

A 7-day cash map is a calendar of money that will actually arrive and bills that will actually leave in the next seven days. It is a timing tool, not a budget personality test.

Why this matters

A month can add up and still fail on Thursday. Rent on the 1st, a payday on the 5th, an insurance draft on the 3rd, and groceries on the weekend are four events. The CFPB bill-calendar tools exist for this reason. FourHundred keeps the idea at seven days because seven days is short enough to finish today.

The $400 test asks whether a small shock becomes a carried balance. The cash map asks whether the next week already has a hole before any shock arrives.

The real-cost example

Take-home $1,900 on Friday the 5th. Rent $1,400 due Monday the 1st. Auto insurance $165 on Wednesday the 3rd. Groceries and gas about $180. Monthly leftover looks like $155. The week still bounces unless rent waited for Friday or cash was sitting there before Monday.

Same dollars. Different clock.

The smallest useful version

Starting cash: what is in checking tonight that you can actually spend. Do not count a credit limit.

Inflows: paycheck, benefits, a transfer already initiated. If it is only hoped for, leave it off.

Outflows you cannot move: rent or mortgage drafts, insurance, scheduled minimums, medicine, transit to work, food.

Running total: add inflows and subtract outflows by day. Circle the first day the total goes below zero.

If a day goes below zero, the honest tools are: move a due date, change when you send rent or a utility if they accept it in writing, or cut an outflow that is not housing, medicine, or work transport. If none of those can happen this week, use the bills path.

Do not invent income. Do not treat a credit card as an inflow.

Common mistakes

  • Using salary instead of the deposit
  • Putting the card limit in the “in” column
  • Mapping 30 days on the first try
  • Cutting food on paper to make Thursday look black
  • Asking a chatbot to optimize the week from a screenshot of six accounts
