Foundation

A 7-day cash map

Last reviewed September 29, 2026 · 8 minutes

Who this is for. Anyone in the U.S. whose month looks possible on paper and still breaks in a particular week.
Who should skip this. Readers who already cannot cover this month’s essentials, or who only need the $400 definition.

Open the one-page sheet — print it or fill it on paper. The sheet is the map. This page is the method.

In one sentence

A 7-day cash map is a calendar of money that will actually arrive and bills that will actually leave in the next seven days. It is a timing tool, not a budget personality test.

Why this matters if money is already tight

A month can add up and still fail on Thursday.

Rent on the 1st, a payday on the 5th, a car insurance draft on the 3rd, and groceries on the weekend are not the same event. They are four events. If you only watch the monthly total, the empty week looks like a mystery.

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau’s bill-calendar tools exist for this reason: due dates and income dates are a separate problem from what you should spend. FourHundred keeps that idea at seven days because seven days is short enough to finish today.

This page does not replace the $400 test. The $400 test asks whether a small shock becomes a carried balance. The cash map asks whether the next week already has a hole before any shock arrives.

The real-cost example

Take-home deposit: $1,900 on Friday the 5th. Rent: $1,400 due Monday the 1st. Auto insurance: $165 drafted Wednesday the 3rd. Groceries and gas before Friday: about $180.

Monthly view. $1,900 − $1,400 − $165 − $180 = $155 leftover. The month works.

The smallest useful version

Draw seven days. Not the rest of the year.

Step 1 — Starting cash. What is in checking tonight that you can actually spend? Do not count pending deposits you have not seen. Do not count a credit limit.

Step 2 — Inflows for the next seven days. Paycheck, benefits, child support that has a history of arriving, a transfer you already initiated. If it is only hoped for, leave it off.

Step 3 — Outflows you cannot move. Rent or mortgage drafts, insurance drafts, minimum payments already scheduled, medicine, transit to work, food you will buy because the week contains weekdays.

Step 4 — Running total. Start with tonight’s cash. Add each inflow on its day. Subtract each outflow on its day. Circle the first day the running total goes below zero.

Step 5 — One change, not five. If a day goes below zero: move a due date; change when you send rent or a utility if they accept it in writing; or cut an outflow that is not housing, medicine, or work transport. If none of those can happen this week, leave the map for the bills path.

Do not invent income. Do not treat a credit card as an inflow.

Labels for an outflow

This page does not rank the month. It only names the tag you write next to a date, so later pages do not invent a new order.

  • Keeps the household housed and able to work. Rent or mortgage, utilities required to stay, food, required medicine, transport required for work.
  • Contract or court minimum. A card minimum, court-ordered support, a tax payment already in enforcement. Current is not the same as paid off.
  • Can move or stop. Subscriptions, a purchase that is not food or medicine, a draft that is not housing or work. Pause only what you can restart.

A collector’s sooner date does not move that bill into the first label. If housing or a shutoff utility is the circled day, leave this map for this month’s bills.

Common mistakes

Using salary instead of the deposit. Taxes, benefits, and wage garnishments already came out. The map only cares what hits the account.

Putting the card limit in the In column. A credit line is not cash. Using it to fill a Tuesday hole is how the growing-balance path starts.

Mapping 30 days on the first try. Thirty days is how this tool dies. Seven days can be finished before the next meal.

Cutting food on paper to make Thursday look black. If you will still buy food, write the food.

Asking an AI chatbot to optimize the week from a screenshot of six accounts. The map is a table you can hold. It does not need a model.

A 20-minute setup

  1. Open the one-page sheet or paper. Write the next seven dates.
  2. Write tonight’s checking balance.
  3. Write each incoming deposit you can name with a date.
  4. Write each required outflow with a date.
  5. Run the total down the page. Circle the first negative day.
  6. Pick one change for that day, or open the bills path.

That is the Foundation. You can repeat it every Sunday. You do not need a new app to start.
