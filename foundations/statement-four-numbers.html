Four numbers on a card statement

Last reviewed October 5, 2026 · 6 minutes

Who this is for. Readers who pay a card and cannot tell which number is the one that stops interest.
Who should skip this. Rent or utilities already at risk this month. Use this month’s bills. A definition is not a payment order.

In one sentence

A card counts as cash or its equivalent in the Fed’s $400 test only if you pay it off at the next statement. The minimum keeps the account current. It does not do that.

The four numbers

Statement balance. What you owed when the cycle closed. This is the amount that, if paid by the due date, is what “paid in full” means on most cards.

Current balance. Statement balance plus charges and minus payments since the cycle closed. It moves. It is not the number the due date is waiting for.

Minimum payment. The amount that keeps the account from being reported late. It is not a payoff. The box on the statement that says how long the minimum would take is a warning, not a plan.

Interest charge. The dollar amount of interest on this statement. If this line is above zero, the balance was not cleared in a way that kept the purchases free of interest.

The grace period is not a fifth number to copy

CFPB defines a grace period as the time between the end of a billing cycle and the due date. Issuers are not required to give one. Most cards give one on purchases. You avoid interest on those purchases only if you pay the balance in full by the due date and you were not already carrying a balance.

If you do not pay in full, interest applies to the unpaid part. New purchases usually start accruing on the day you make them. You can also lose the grace period for the next cycle. Cash advances generally have no grace period and accrue from the day of the advance.

One small action

From one statement, copy the four numbers and the due date. Then write one line: I paid the minimum, or I paid the statement balance.

Do not calculate months-to-payoff on this page. Why the minimum leaves the balance alive is a Playbook. Stopping new charges for one cycle is a different Playbook.

What this page does not do

  • It does not tell you which card to pay first.
  • It does not count a carried balance as the cash in the $400 test. That definition stays on the $400 page.
  • It does not ask for an account login.
