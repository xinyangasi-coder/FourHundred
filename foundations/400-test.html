The $400 test and the three cash buffers

Last reviewed September 29, 2026 · 9 minutes

Who this is for. Anyone in the U.S. who would have to think twice about a few-hundred-dollar surprise.
Who should skip this. Readers whose cash buffer already covers several months of essential bills.

In one sentence

The $400 test asks whether you could cover a sudden $400 expense with cash, savings, or a card you pay off at the next statement. It is a measure of a thin buffer, not a complete emergency fund.

Why this matters if money is already tight

A tight month usually does not fail on rent day. It fails on the unplanned bill that arrives after rent is gone.

The Federal Reserve SHED, fielded in October 2025 and published in May 2026, asked U.S. adults how they would pay a hypothetical $400 emergency expense.

  • 63 percent would cover it exclusively with cash or its equivalent: cash, savings, or a credit card paid in full at the next statement.
  • 37 percent would borrow, sell something, carry a card balance, use a mix of those, or not pay.
  • 12 percent of all adults would be unable to pay the expense by any means.

That 63 percent figure was unchanged from 2022–2024 and below 68 percent in 2021. If $400 would become a balance you carry, the next surprise is more expensive than $400.

The real-cost example

A $400 car repair on a Wednesday: cash or a card paid at the next statement costs $400. A carried balance at 22 percent APR is no longer a one-time $400 event. If you cannot pay, the $400 problem becomes a work, childcare, or collections problem.

In the same survey, 59 percent of adults had at least one major unexpected expense in the prior 12 months. Vehicle repair 30 percent, house or appliance 22 percent, medical 21 percent. Those events are larger than $400. That is why $400 is a floor test, not a savings goal.

The three rungs

Rung 1 — $400. A separate place that can absorb one small shock without a carried card balance.

Rung 2 — one month of essential bills. Housing, utilities, food, transport, minimum debt payments, insurance you cannot pause.

Rung 3 — three months of those same essentials. In 2025, 55 percent of adults said they had set aside three months of expenses. That is a different question from $400. Do not mix the two percentages.

If you cannot fund all three, fund Rung 1 first. If moving money this week would bounce rent, do not move it.

Common mistakes

  • Treating “I have a credit card” as cash. A card counts only if you pay it off at the next statement.
  • Mixing surveys. $1,000 polls and “paycheck to paycheck” are not the $400 test.
  • Parking the buffer in the same checking account as rent.
  • Skipping Rung 1 because Rung 3 feels impossible.
  • Using a payday loan or advance app as the buffer.

When this is not enough

If you already cannot pay this month’s rent, utilities, or a collection notice, use Start here: I may not make this month’s bills.

Next action

If I had a $400 emergency expense tomorrow, I would pay it by ________.

Move one transfer today only if it does not threaten rent, food, medicine, or a required minimum payment.

Sources: Federal Reserve, Economic Well-Being of U.S. Households in 2025.
