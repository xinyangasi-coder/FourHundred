The $400 test and the three cash buffers

Last reviewed September 29, 2026 · 9 minutes

Who this is for. Anyone in the U.S. who would have to think twice about a few-hundred-dollar surprise.
Who should skip this. Readers whose cash buffer already covers several months of essential bills.

In one sentence

The $400 test asks whether you could cover a sudden $400 expense with cash, savings, or a card you pay off at the next statement. It is a measure of a thin buffer, not a complete emergency fund.

Why this matters if money is already tight

A tight month usually does not fail on rent day. It fails on the unplanned bill that arrives after rent is gone.

The Federal Reserve’s SHED, fielded in October 2025 and published in May 2026: 63 percent would cover $400 exclusively with cash or its equivalent; 37 percent would not; 12 percent could not pay by any means. Unchanged from 2022–2024, below 68 percent in 2021.

The real-cost example

Path A — cash or a card paid at the next statement. The repair costs $400.

Path B — carry the balance. Interest starts.

Path C — cannot pay. The $400 problem becomes a work or collections problem.

59 percent of adults had at least one major unexpected expense in the prior 12 months. Vehicle repair was the most common (30 percent). $400 is a floor test, not a savings goal.

Three rungs

Rung 1 — $400. Rung 2 — one month of essentials. Rung 3 — three months (a different Fed question: 55 percent in 2025). If moving money this week would bounce rent, do not move it.
