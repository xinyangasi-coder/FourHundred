Three months of essentials is not the $400 test

Last reviewed October 5, 2026 · 6 minutes

Who this is for. Readers whose rent and utilities are covered this month and who need the words for a longer buffer.
Who should skip this. A 7-day cash map that would go negative, or rent and utilities already at risk. Use this month’s bills. A reserve target is not this week’s payment order.

In one sentence

Rung 3 is three months of essential bills, set aside where daily spending will not take it. It is a different question from whether a $400 shock can be paid with cash or a card paid off at the next statement.

What the Fed actually asked

In the 2025 Survey of Household Economics and Decisionmaking, 55 percent of adults said they had set aside money for three months of expenses in an emergency or “rainy day” fund. That share was unchanged from 2024 and down from 59 percent in 2021.

That is a self-report. It is not a bank balance the Fed checked. It is also not the same as “I could cover three months if I borrowed or sold something.” Another 15 percent of adults said they could cover three months that way. 30 percent said they could not cover three months by any of those means.

The $400 figure is a different question: 63 percent would cover a hypothetical $400 expense with cash, savings, or a card paid off at the next statement. Do not treat 55 and 63 as two readings of the same fund. Definitions stay on the $400 test page.

Essentials, not the current lifestyle

For this page, one month of essentials is the money that keeps the household housed and able to work:

  • Rent or mortgage, plus insurance and tax that keep that housing
  • Electric, gas, water, and the heat or cooling required to stay
  • Food and required medicine
  • Transport required for work
  • Court-ordered support or another legal obligation already in force

A car repair is often larger than $400. In the same 2025 survey, 30 percent of adults had a major vehicle repair or replacement in the prior year. That is why rung 1 is a floor test. It does not mean this page is an insurance guide.

One small action

Write one month of the list above. Multiply by three. Then write where that money is now, and whether a normal card swipe can reach it.

If moving money this week would bounce rent, do not move it. The number can wait. The housing payment cannot.

What this page does not do

  • It does not rank this month’s bills. That order lives on Start here.
  • It does not say three months is the amount you should have. 55 percent is a survey share, not a target assigned to you.
  • It does not count a carried card balance as the reserve.

Sources

Fed SHED 2025, Savings and Investments — 55 percent rainy-day fund; 15 percent could cover three months by borrowing, selling, or other savings; 30 percent could not.
