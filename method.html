What FourHundred is

FourHundred is a U.S. money information tool for people already under financial pressure. It is published by Xinyang Chen. See About.

The name comes from a question the Federal Reserve has asked U.S. adults for years: if an unexpected $400 expense arrived, how would you pay it? That question is a public benchmark for a thin cash buffer. We use it as a starting point, not as a verdict.

Three layers: Foundations change slowly. Playbook holds current methods. Evidence holds numbers from primary sources, with the definition next to the figure.

What a page is supposed to do

A Foundation page gives shared language and one small action. A Playbook page gives one method, who it is for, and who should skip it. An Evidence card gives a number, the exact definition, and what the number does not prove. If a page cannot name its job in one sentence, it is not ready to publish.

How we choose what to publish

  • Useful to someone already under pressure
  • Explainable with a dollar example
  • Does not require a full income file
  • Has a named limit: who should not use it
  • Facts point to a primary source, or they are labeled as examples
  • Does not recommend a specific bank, card, loan, investment, or filing

How the three layers stay different

Foundations are revised, not chased. Review monthly, or sooner if a law or official definition changes.

Playbook is reviewed every Monday. A new method ships that day only if it meets the rules on this page. If it does not, Home and the Playbook index keep last week’s method and say so. Old methods stay up if they are still accurate. They are dated.

Evidence updates when the source updates. We do not invent a weekly statistic by rewriting last year’s number.

Home This week carries one item from each layer when a layer has a live page. That keeps Foundations from turning into news.

How we treat data and AI

Every Evidence card includes the number, the date, the publisher, a link, the definition, who is in the number, and what it does not prove. Cash or its equivalent in the Fed’s $400 question means cash, savings, or a card paid off at the next statement.

AI can help a reader understand a term already on FourHundred, compare two published methods, or draft questions. It should not pick a product, receive live bank access, or be treated as a source. Pages are written by people.

What we will not do

  • Give personalized financial, tax, or legal advice
  • Rank or sell financial products
  • Promise a method will retire debt or raise a score
  • Treat a chatbot answer as a source
