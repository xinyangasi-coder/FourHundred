Plain title

Share of U.S. adults who would cover a $400 emergency with cash or its equivalent

The number

63 percent

The complement is 37 percent who would borrow, sell something, carry a card balance, use a mix of those, or not pay. 12 percent of all adults said they would be unable to pay the expense by any means.

The same figure across surveys

68 percent in the 2021 survey. 63 percent in 2022, 2023, 2024, and 2025. The line starts at zero so a flat 63 is not drawn as a drop.

63 percent is cash, savings, or a card paid at the next statement. 37 percent is not “no money.” 12 percent said they could not pay by any means. Survey years, not release years.

Date and source

  • Survey: Federal Reserve Board, Survey of Household Economics and Decisionmaking (SHED)
  • Field period: October 17–28, 2025
  • Report: Economic Well-Being of U.S. Households in 2025, released May 13, 2026

Exact definition

The Fed asked how an adult would pay a hypothetical $400 unexpected expense.

Cash or its equivalent means the person would cover it exclusively with cash, savings, or a credit card paid off at the next statement.

Putting the $400 on a card and carrying the balance is not cash or its equivalent.

Who is in the number, who is not

  • In the number: U.S. adults in the 2025 SHED.
  • The 63 percent are people who said the $400 would be handled with cash-equivalent means only.
  • The 37 percent is everyone else in that question, grouped together. It is not the same thing as 37 percent have zero dollars.
  • The 12 percent is a subset: adults who said they could not pay the $400 by any means.
  • This is not Bankrate’s $1,000 question, a paycheck-to-paycheck poll, or the separate three-month rainy-day question (55 percent in 2025).

What this does not prove

  • It does not prove that 37 percent of adults are broke.
  • It does not prove that 63 percent are financially healthy.
  • It does not measure a full emergency fund. $400 is a small-shock test.
  • It does not tell you what you should transfer today.
