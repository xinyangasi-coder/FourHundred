Plain title

Share of U.S. households that say they have a bit more or far more debt than is manageable

The number

31 percent

The complement is 69 percent who said they have a manageable amount of debt or no debt.

Date and source

  • Survey: Financial Health Pulse, Financial Health Network
  • Field period: spring 2026
  • Report: Financial Health Pulse 2026 U.S. Trends Report, September 2026
  • Sample: nationally representative households from USC’s Understanding America Study. Whole-sample N = 7,662. This indicator used a split sample (N = 3,812).

Pulse 2026 PDF

Pulse data page

Exact definition

Pulse asked households to think about all current debts, including mortgages, bank loans, student loans, money owed to people, medical debt, past-due bills, and credit-card balances carried over from prior months.

The published 31 percent is the share who chose “Have a bit more debt than is manageable” or “Have far more debt than is manageable.”

The denominator includes households with no debt. Question wording changed in 2026; the year-to-year chart uses the old wording. This is a household survey, not the Fed SHED.

Who is in the number, who is not

  • In the number: U.S. households in Pulse who described total debt as a bit more or far more than manageable.
  • Not a count of accounts in collections or missed payments last month.
  • Not the Fed $400 question.
  • Not “31 percent of adults.”

Student-loan borrowers: 55 percent, up from 50 percent. Auto-loan borrowers: 40 percent, up from 36 percent. Those cuts are not the national 31 percent.

The report calls 31 percent the highest reading in nine Pulse years. Related Pulse figures: Financially Vulnerable 17 percent; paid all bills on time over 12 months 68 percent; Financially Healthy 31 percent. Do not add those percentages together.

What this does not prove

  • It does not prove that 31 percent of households are in collections or in default.
  • It does not prove a dollar amount of debt.
  • It does not equal the Fed SHED finding that 16 percent of adults did not pay all bills last month.
  • It does not tell you which of your bills to pay today.
