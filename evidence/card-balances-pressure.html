Layer: Evidence · Last reviewed: September 29, 2026 · Card ID: EV-SHED-CC-WELLBEING-2025

Plain title

Average credit-card balance change, 2023 to 2025, among SHED respondents with cards who said they were “finding it difficult to get by”

The number

+$2,530

That is a 37 percent rise, from $6,735 in 2023 to $9,265 in 2025. Adults “living comfortably” saw +$59. The overall matched-sample average rose +$748.

Date and source

  • Survey: Federal Reserve Board, SHED 2025
  • Report: Economic Well-Being of U.S. Households in 2025, Credit chapter, Table A
  • Method: survey answers matched to credit-record balances. The matched file does not include names, addresses, or account numbers.

https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2026-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2025-credit.htm

PDF

Exact definition

Table A is limited to SHED respondents who had credit cards and consented to matching. Financial well-being is the person’s 2025 answer. Average card balances for that same person are compared in 2023 and 2025.

  • Living comfortably — 34 percent of adults
  • Doing okay — 39 percent
  • Just getting by — 19 percent
  • Finding it difficult to get by — 8 percent

“Finding it difficult” plus “just getting by” accounted for 65 percent of card-balance growth in the 2025 matched comparison, versus about 40 percent in 2023 and 2024 comparisons.

Not in the $2,530 figure: people without cards, people who did not consent to the match, NY Fed total revolving debt, or Pulse unmanageable-debt households.

What this does not prove

  • It does not prove that every person in that group added $2,530. It is a group average.
  • It does not prove the extra balance came only from new spending.
  • It does not tell you your APR or minimum payment.
  • It does not rank issuers.
