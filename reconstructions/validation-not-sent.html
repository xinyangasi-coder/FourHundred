Reconstruction · Late, or in collections

The validation notice was not sent

Filed October 6, 2026 · A 2015 order · Not a person to copy

What this is. A named enforcement order, plus the current CFPB rule on a written dispute.
Who should skip this. A summons. Stop. This page is not a court response.

Situation

This file serves I am late, or in collections. The paper is a collector’s notice, or the absence of one.

The shock

On June 18, 2015, the CFPB ordered Syndicated Office Systems, a medical-debt collector, to provide over $5.4 million in relief and pay a $500,000 penalty. The order said the company failed to send debt validation notices to more than 10,000 consumers.

How the number changed

The same order said the company failed to respond to more than 13,000 credit-report disputes within the 30 days the law required. The average response took more than 90 days. Some took more than a year. Disputes were handled like ordinary complaints, with no deadline.

The missing notice did not erase the bill. It removed the paper a person needed in order to dispute on time.

The fork

CFPB’s current page, last reviewed December 9, 2024, still says a written dispute generally within 30 days of the first communication requires the collector to stop collection until it sends verification. Collection can restart after that verification. A dispute is not proof the debt is not owed.

One step already on this site

If a notice arrived, use the validation method and its sheet. Four lines, then a CFPB sample only if the amount or the creditor is wrong. This 2015 order does not add a new letter.

Do not borrow this

The $5.4 million and the penalty followed an agency investigation. They are not a method. The order is eleven years old. It shows a failure mode, not the name of a collector to call.

Sources and date

CFPB, can a collector collect after a dispute — last reviewed December 9, 2024.
