Reconstruction · A growing card balance

The payment was applied in the wrong order

Filed October 6, 2026 · Exam findings, not a named household · Not a person to copy

What this is. Auto-loan exam findings about a payment that did not land where the notice said. Not a payoff story.
Who should skip this. Readers who want a payoff order. Use the minimum-payment method instead.

Situation

This file serves My credit card balance is growing only at the fork where a payment and the reported amount disagree. The exams themselves were auto loans, not card statements. The mechanism is the same kind: the payment exists, the record does not match it.

The shock

In the same Fall 2024 Supervisory Highlights, examiners found servicers applying auto-loan payments in a different order than the order disclosed on the servicer’s website. Borrowers were charged late fees that followed from that order. Examiners directed refunds of those fees.

How the number changed

The same edition found inaccurate loan information on thousands of credit reports: wrong amounts past due on charged-off accounts, wrong dates of falling behind, and wrong payment amounts after a payoff or settlement. A paid amount and the reported amount were not the same number.

That is not interest from a minimum payment. Interest is the other path, already on the minimum-payment method. This file is the record being wrong.

The fork

If the statement balance is rising while you pay at least the minimum, the open question is still interest and new charges. If the reported past-due amount is larger than the payment you can document, the open question is the furnisher’s record. Do not treat the two as one problem.

One step already on this site

For a rising card balance, use one cycle with no new charges. For a collector’s notice about that balance, use the validation sheet. This file does not add a dispute letter.

Do not borrow this

The refunds were an exam direction. A reader cannot cite “Issue 35” as their own case. The findings do not say a card issuer must apply a card payment the way an auto servicer’s website described.

Source and date

CFPB, October 7, 2024 — Supervisory Highlights, Issue 35. Payment order, late fees, and inaccurate furnishing. Unnamed servicers.
