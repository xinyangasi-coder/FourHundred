The $400 test is a small-shock test, not a full emergency fund.

### 2 — Method of the week
Kicker  
Playbook

Title  
The minimum payment keeps the account current. It does not retire the balance.

Line  
On a $2,000 balance at 22 percent APR, a typical first minimum puts most of the payment on interest. Use the warning box on your own statement before any example, including ours.

Link  
Read the Playbook → /playbook/minimum-payment

### 3 — Number people misread
Kicker  
Evidence

Title  
37 percent would not use cash or its equivalent. That is not the same as 37 percent having zero dollars.

Line  
12 percent of adults said they could not pay $400 by any means. The rest of that 37 percent would borrow, sell something, or carry a card balance. Definitions stay on the card.

## How FourHundred is built
Three short columns, no icons required.

Foundations  
Stay stable. We revise them when a definition or a law changes. Start with the $400 test.

Playbook  
Updates weekly. One method, with who it is for and who should skip it. This week: minimum payments.

Evidence  
Updates when the source updates. We keep $400, $1,000, and “paycheck to paycheck” on separate cards.

## Designer notes

- Situation buttons are the hero. Do not put gauges above the chooser.  
- “This week” is three text cards, not a blog feed.  
- Each gauge must show Number + Unit + one plain line + source + date. Hover or tap opens the definition, not a stock chart.  
- Gauge 2 and Pulse’s 12-month bill question must never share a headline.  
- Buttons 2 and 6 are aliased until their own Start here pages exist. Do not invent a third destination.  
- No smiling-couple photography. No live index prices.
