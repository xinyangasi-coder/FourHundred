What this is

FourHundred is a U.S. money information tool for people already under financial pressure.

The name comes from a Federal Reserve question: if an unexpected $400 expense arrived, how would you pay it? That question is a public benchmark for a thin cash buffer. It is a starting point, not a verdict on anyone’s life.

Pages are organized in three layers:

  • Foundations — core knowledge that changes slowly
  • Playbook — current methods, with limits written on the page
  • Evidence — numbers from primary sources, with the definition next to the figure

Start here is the front door when the month is already tight. Method explains how pages are chosen. AI tools explains what a model may and may not do here.

Who publishes it

FourHundred is an independent education project at fourhundredx.com. It is written for adults in the United States.

It is not a bank, a credit counselor, a law firm, a registered investment adviser, or a debt-settlement company. Launch pages do not sell products and do not take referral fees.

Editorial rules live on the Method page. Privacy describes what the current static site does and does not collect.

What we will not do

  • Give personalized financial, tax, or legal advice
  • Rank or sell financial products
  • Promise that a method will get someone out of debt
  • Ask for a Social Security number, a bank login, or a live account connection
  • Treat a chatbot answer as a source

How to flag an error

If a number, definition, date, or link is wrong, email:

corrections@fourhundredx.com

That address is for corrections and broken links. It is not a counseling line, a hardship application, or a place to send your file.

A useful note includes:

  • the page address
  • the sentence or figure that looks wrong
  • the source you think we missed, if you have one

Do not put any of these in the email:

  • a Social Security number
  • an account number, routing number, or password
  • a full statement, a summons, or a medical bill with identifiers
  • a request that we tell you which bill to pay or whether to file

If the note is a real error, the page gets a correction line: old text, new text, and the date of the change. We would rather show a scar than a silent edit.

Mail to that address is not confidential legal or financial advice. If you need a person for a court date, an eviction, or a collector, use the official links on the relevant Start here page, 211, or legal aid.
