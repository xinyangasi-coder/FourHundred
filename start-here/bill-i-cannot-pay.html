I got a bill I cannot pay

Last reviewed September 29, 2026 · 30–45 minutes

Who this is for. Adults in the United States who received one bill — medical, vehicle, tax, utility lump, or similar — that will not fit this month, while the rest of the month is still mostly standing.
Who should skip this. People who cannot cover several essentials this month. Use I may not make this month’s bills. If the bill is already with a collector or a court, use I am late, or in collections.

What this page is for

One large bill and a month that cannot cover every bill are different problems. This page is for the first one.

The job is: name the bill, check that it is yours and that the amount is real, keep housing and food standing, then ask the sender for a path that does not invent a new high-cost loan.

FourHundred does not know whether the charge is correct, whether charity care applies, or what your state will do if you miss it. A notice with a deadline still beats a clean theory.

Leave this page if any of these are true

  • The unpaid item is this month’s rent or mortgage itself
  • Several essentials are already short, not just this one bill
  • The paper says collector, lawsuit, garnishment, or a court date
  • The car that gets you to work is days from repossession

Those belong on the other Start here pages.

Do this first (15 minutes)

  1. Write the sender, the amount, the due date, and the account or invoice number. Do not copy a Social Security number onto a new sheet if it is already on the bill.
  2. Ask: is this bill mine, and is the amount the final amount after insurance, a prior payment, or a discount?
  3. Ask: if I paid nothing this week, what actually happens — a late fee, a shutoff, a collector, or a court paper?
  4. Ask: would paying it in full this week bounce rent, utilities, food, or required medicine?

If line 4 is yes, the bill waits behind the protect-first list. Paying a hospital statement while the lease breaks is not discipline. It is a worse month.

A working order for one unpaid bill

1. Confirm the charge before you finance it. Ask for an itemized statement if the total is a lump.

2. If it is a medical bill, ask about help before you ask about a loan. CFPB’s Ask CFPB pages treat nonprofit-hospital financial assistance — often called charity care — as a first question. Nonprofit hospitals under the Affordable Care Act are required to have a financial-assistance policy and to give you a copy free of charge. Ask how long the application takes, and what happens to the bill while it is pending.

If you have insurance and the bill looks like an out-of-network surprise after emergency or certain in-network care, the federal No Surprises Act may cap what you owe. Ground ambulance is often outside that protection. CMS runs a No Surprises help desk at 1-800-985-3059.

3. Ask the original sender for a path you can actually keep. Name an amount and a date you can send without bouncing rent. Write the date, the name, and what they said. Do not authorize a one-time debit if you do not know the day it will hit.

4. Keep the protect-first list above this invoice. Housing, essential utilities, food, required medicine, and the transport that keeps the job sit above a surprise bill that has a slower consequence.

5. If a collector already has it, change rooms. Collector rules live on the collections path.

What not to do this week

  • Do not take a payday loan, title loan, or cash-advance app to make one invoice look current while rent stays unpaid.
  • Do not put a medical bill on a new high-interest card as the first move, before you have asked for an itemized statement and for financial assistance.
  • Do not ignore a date that names shutoff, eviction, or a court appearance.
  • Do not send a full statement, an account number, or a Social Security number to a public chatbot.
  • Do not promise an amount that you already know will bounce.

Why this bill showed up

In Fed SHED 2025, 59 percent of U.S. adults had at least one major unexpected expense in the prior 12 months. Thirty percent had a major vehicle repair or replacement. A missing $400 cash buffer is how that repair becomes a statement balance.

If you still want a chatbot

A public chatbot is not this page and not your statement. Do not paste account numbers, and do not ask it which bill to pay or whether to file. After this path is named, use AI tools for the boundary and Filter a money question for a 15-minute script — nothing else.
