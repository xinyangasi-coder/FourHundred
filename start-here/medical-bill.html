I have a hospital bill I cannot pay

Last reviewed October 5, 2026 · 6 minutes

Who this is for. Adults in the United States with a hospital or clinic bill, or a collector calling about one, who cannot pay it in full this month.
Who should skip this. A paper that says summons or complaint. Stop and use legal aid. This page is not a court response.

Name the paper first

  • A bill from the hospital or clinic, not yet with a collector.
  • A collector’s validation notice about a medical bill.
  • A court paper. Stop. Do not use the steps below as an answer to a lawsuit.

A medical bill is still a bill. Asking for a list of charges does not erase it. A charity-care application does not erase it until the hospital says the application was approved.

What to do this week

  1. Ask the provider for an itemized bill: dates, services, and amounts. CFPB calls a detailed list a superbill in its medical-debt advisory. Do not pay a bill you do not recognize in order to make the calls stop.
  2. If it is a hospital, ask for the financial assistance policy, sometimes called charity care, and the application. Nonprofit hospitals must have a written policy and give a plain-language summary. Ask for a free copy. A for-profit hospital is not under that same federal tax rule; ask anyway, and write down what they say.
  3. Apply with the income papers they ask for. Ask how long a decision takes, and what happens to the bill while you wait.
  4. If a collector is already on this bill, tell them in writing that a financial-assistance application is pending and ask them to pause collection while it is. CFPB publishes a sample letter for that. Pausing contact is not the same as the debt disappearing.
  5. If the paper is a validation notice, write the four lines before you answer. Use the validation method and its sheet. Do not send a chatbot letter.

Two federal limits, not a state map

For care on or after January 1, 2022, the No Surprises Act can limit some emergency bills and some out-of-network bills at an in-network facility. It does not cover every surprise amount. If you had no insurance, or you did not use insurance, the provider generally had to give a good-faith estimate beforehand. CFPB notes that if the bill is $400 or more above that estimate, you may be able to use the patient-provider dispute process. Check the date of care and the estimate before you rely on that.

State charity-care rules differ. This page does not list them. The hospital’s own policy, and your state’s page, outrank a general article.

What not to do

  • Put the hospital bill on a card so the collector stops, if that card cannot be paid at the next statement.
  • Ignore a court date because an application is “in process.”
  • Give a Social Security number, a bank login, or a one-time debit authorization on the first call.

Methods for this path

  • Read the validation notice before you answer a collector
  • I am late, or in collections — if the paper is already past the hospital
  • I may not make this month’s bills — if rent or utilities are also short
  • A $59 clinic bill became a lawsuit — a public record of the cost after a case is filed. Not a method.

If you still want a chatbot

Do not paste the bill, the account number, or a diagnosis. After this path is named, use AI tools for the boundary. A chatbot does not know whether this hospital is nonprofit.
