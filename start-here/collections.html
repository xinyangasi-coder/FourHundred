I am late, or in collections

Last reviewed October 1, 2026 · 30–40 minutes

Who this is for. Adults in the United States who missed a payment, received a collection call or letter, or saw an account leave the original company.
Who should skip this. People whose accounts are current, or who already have a lawyer handling a case.

What this page is for

Late and in collections are not the same stage.

  • Late with the original company means the card issuer, hospital, landlord, or servicer still owns the account.
  • In collections usually means another firm is trying to collect, or the original company has moved the account to a collections department.
  • In court means a summons, complaint, or garnishment paper exists. That is a deadline, not a phone problem.

This page helps you name the stage, keep housing and food first, write things down, and use official CFPB rights pages. It is not legal advice. It does not tell you to ignore a debt you owe. It does not tell you which debt to pay.

If today you cannot cover rent, utilities, food, or medicine, use Start here: I may not make this month’s bills in the same sitting. Collection noise is not a reason to skip shelter.

Name the stage (10 minutes)

For each overdue account, write: original company; who is calling or writing now; amount they name; date of first collection letter or call you can prove; any paper that says summons, complaint, judgment, garnishment, eviction, or foreclosure.

Then circle one stage: still with the original company; a collector is contacting me; court or lockout paper is here. If more than one account is late, do not blend them.

If you are late with the original company

Pay nothing extra toward this account until housing, essential utilities, food, and required medicine for this month are covered. If cash remains: send the minimum on time if you can; call the original company; ask whether a hardship program, a due-date change, or a written arrangement exists; write the date, the name, and what they said.

If a collector is contacting you

Collectors generally have to give you validation information when they first communicate, or within five days of first contact. Read the paper before you talk.

  • If you do not recognize the debt, or the amount looks wrong, CFPB says you can dispute in writing within 30 days of receiving the validation information.
  • If you do recognize the debt, a dispute is not a strategy for delay.
  • Keep copies. Do not give a one-time debit code on the first call if you do not understand when it will hit.

Collectors may not harass you. A CFPB complaint is not a payment plan. Do not send money to a company that promises to wipe the debt for a fee before you have read CFPB pages on debt relief.

If court paper is here

Stop using this page as the main tool. A summons or complaint has a response deadline. Ignoring it can turn into a default judgment.

  1. Read the deadline on the paper
  2. Contact legal aid or a legal-services office in your area
  3. If the paper is about housing, also call 211
  4. Keep paying the protect-first bills if you can

Do not take a payday loan to make the lawsuit go away on a phone promise.

What not to do

  • Do not hide from a summons.
  • Do not pay the loudest caller first just because they called twice.
  • Do not share a Social Security number, account password, or live bank login with a number you did not initiate.
  • Do not ask a public AI chatbot whether you should file bankruptcy or which collector to pay.
  • Do not treat I disputed it as the same thing as I do not owe it.

Methods for this path

  • Read the validation notice before you answer a collector
  • The validation notice was not sent — a 2015 order. Not a method.
  • Make one hardship call and write down the answer

If you still want a chatbot

A public chatbot is not this page and not your statement. Do not paste account numbers, and do not ask it which bill to pay or whether to file. After this path is named, use AI tools for the boundary and Filter a money question for a 15-minute script — nothing else.

If you already have the letters, a model may sort the dates you typed: who wrote, what amount they named, what deadline is printed. It may not invent a state law or a letter that has legal effect. The letter you send still comes from CFPB’s collector pages, not from the chat.

Next action (today)

Pick the worst paper in the pile — the one with the nearest deadline. Write four lines: stage; amount named; deadline on the page, if any; first official link I will open today. Then do one thing only.
