I may not make this month’s bills

Last reviewed September 29, 2026 · 30–45 minutes today

Who this is for. Adults in the United States who already know this month’s money may not cover every bill.
Who should skip this. People whose bills are current and who only need a buffer or interest explanation.

Open the one-page sheet — print it or fill it on paper. The sheet is today’s list. This page is the method.

What this page is for

This is a path for this month, not a plan for the rest of your financial life. The job is: keep shelter, power, food, medicine, and the ability to earn a paycheck, then decide what can wait.

You are still responsible for every bill. When the cash is not enough for every bill on time, the order you pay in changes the damage. FourHundred does not know your lease, your state shutoff rules, or your court dates. If eviction, foreclosure, utility shutoff, repossession, or a lawsuit is already in motion, use the official links and get a person — legal aid, a housing counselor, or 211 — the same day.

Do this first (10 minutes)

Use the sheet or paper. This month only. Write:

  1. Cash and money that will actually arrive before the next due date
  2. Bills already due or due in the next 14 days
  3. Which of those bills, if unpaid, would take housing, heat/cooling/water, medicine, or the way you get to work
  4. Any letter that mentions eviction, foreclosure, shutoff, repossession, wage garnishment, or a court date

If line 4 is not empty, that item jumps the list.

A working order when cash cannot cover everything

Starting order, then change it if a notice, a state rule, or a court order says otherwise.

Protect first: housing; essential utilities; food and required medicine; transport and insurance required to keep a job or required care; court-ordered payments such as child support, and tax debts that already have enforcement.

Keep current if anything is left: minimum payments on accounts that are still open and not in collections.

Usually later: extra principal on cards or personal loans; subscriptions that can be paused without losing housing, work, or medicine.

A collector can sound urgent. An eviction notice is a different category. If losing the car means losing the job that pays the rent, write that exception on the sheet. Do not let a phone call write it for you.

What to do with each unpaid bill

Call or write before the due date if you still can. The sheet has words you can read: the month is short, the amount you can actually send, the date, and whether a hardship or arrangement exists. Write the name, the date, and what they said. Do not promise an amount that would bounce rent. Do not give a debit authorization you do not understand.

Housing and utility help are local. Call 211. Energy help often starts at LIHEAP or the National Energy Assistance Referral line, 866-674-6327. Assistance can take weeks. If shutoff or eviction is close, say that in the first sentence.

What not to do this month

  • Do not take a payday loan, title loan, or get-cash-today app to make a card look current while rent stays unpaid.
  • Do not empty a retirement account to clear unsecured bills without a tax professional looking at the cost.
  • Do not pay the loudest collector first just because they called twice.
  • Do not connect live bank accounts to a public AI chatbot and ask it which bill to pay.
  • Do not ignore a court summons.

Next action (today)

Finish the three columns on the sheet. Send or schedule the first protect-first payment that is still possible. Make the first call on the first unpaid essential bill.
