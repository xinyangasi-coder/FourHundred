My credit card balance is growing

Last reviewed September 29, 2026 · 25–40 minutes

Who this is for. Adults in the United States whose card balance is higher than last month, or who keep paying and watch the number stay put.
Who should skip this. People who already pay in full, or whose rent and utilities are at risk this month.

What this page is for

This path is for a balance that is alive: it grows, or it fails to fall, even when you pay. FourHundred does not recommend an issuer, a balance-transfer offer, or a consolidation loan.

If paying this card’s minimum would bounce rent, utilities, food, or medicine, leave this page and use I may not make this month’s bills.

Do this first

Write: statement balance, minimum due and due date, purchase APR, new charges this cycle, interest and fees this cycle. Then open the Minimum Payment Warning box. Federal rules require the issuer to show years at the minimum, estimated total cost, and a 36-month comparison payment.

Why the number can rise while you pay on time

New spending on a revolving balance. Interest larger than the part of the payment that hits principal. Fees. The Fed’s 2025 SHED, matched to credit records, found adults “finding it difficult to get by” saw average card balances rise about $2,530 from 2023 to 2025. Adults “living comfortably” saw +$59.

A working order

  1. Pay at least the minimum on time if cash remains after housing and food.
  2. Stop adding new purchases to this card if you can.
  3. Read the 36-month comparison payment. If it fits after essentials, use it. If not, stay at the minimum.
  4. Call the issuer only with a number you can keep.
  5. Do not open a second problem to look busy.

Official: CFPB credit cards · Reg Z minimum-payment warning
