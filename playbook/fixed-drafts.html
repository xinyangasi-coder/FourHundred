Mark the drafts that hit before payday

Last reviewed October 1, 2026 · 15 minutes

Who this is for. Adults in the United States who earn enough over the month and still hit zero before payday.
Who should skip this. Anyone who may miss rent or a shutoff utility — use this month’s bills. Anyone looking for a 50/30/20 budget. This page does not assign percents.

The situation is not “you spend wrong.” It is a timing gap: money leaves before the next deposit. This method only marks that gap.

The method in one paragraph

List every automatic draft between today and the next payday: rent, insurance, subscriptions, card minimums, buy-now-pay-later. Circle only items that can pause without losing housing, the way you get to work, or required medicine. For a circled subscription, cancel it in the company’s own site. For a circled card date, use the due-date ask. Do not open a new account to “smooth” the week.

Steps

  1. Next deposit date: ____. Cash until then: $____.
  2. Write each draft: name, amount, date. Round numbers are enough.
  3. Star housing, utilities, insurance you need to drive to work, and medicine. Those are not the pause list.
  4. Circle the unstarred drafts. Pause or cancel those in the biller’s own account. Write the confirmation number.
  5. If a card minimum is the item that overdraws the week and rent is covered, use the due-date method, not a new card.

A worked example (labeled as an example)

Payday on Friday. $140 in the account. A $15 streaming draft on Wednesday and a $47 card minimum on Thursday. The $15 can be paused. The $47 is a minimum, not a subscription. The example is not your statement. See I earn enough and still run out.

What can go wrong

  • Canceling car insurance because it drafted on a short week.
  • Connecting a bank login to a chatbot so it can “find subscriptions.”
  • Treating a percent budget as the fix when the problem is the day of the draft.

Next action

One week. One circled draft you can pause. Confirmation written down. Stop.
