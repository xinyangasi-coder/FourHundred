Playbook · Lookup · October 1, 2026

Search official unclaimed property, then your own old logins

Last reviewed October 1, 2026 · 20 minutes

Who this is for. Adults in the United States who have moved, changed jobs, or changed banks, and whose housing for this month is already handled.
Who should skip this. Anyone in a shutoff or eviction week — do the bills path first. Anyone about to pay a company that contacted them about “unclaimed money.”

This is a lookup, not a windfall plan. State programs returned $4.49 billion in fiscal 2024. Claims paid through MissingMoney averaged $2,080; the median was $100. Most finds are small. The search is free.

The method in one paragraph

Search your name at MissingMoney.com or your state treasurer’s unclaimed-property page. Do not pay a finder. Then log in yourself to an old employer retirement plan, Venmo or similar, and your state tax site. Do not upload those screenshots to a chatbot. Write what you found, including zero.

Steps

  1. Search MissingMoney.com and your state treasurer. Hawaii is not in the shared database; use that state’s own page if you lived there.
  2. If a private letter offers to recover the money for a fee, stop. The state search is free. NAUPA says there is no cost to claim through the official program.
  3. On your own phone, open old payroll, brokerage, and payment apps you already remember. Do not type passwords into a chat.
  4. Write: source; amount shown or “none”; claim number if any. A median of $100 is the planning number, not the average.

What can go wrong

  • Paying an upfront recovery fee.
  • Treating a news story about $10,000 as the typical claim.
  • Pasting the claim screen into a public chatbot “to see if it is real.”

Next action

One official search. One old login. Stop after 20 minutes. Come back next month if you remember another employer.
